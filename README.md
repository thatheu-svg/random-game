A top-down survival game written in Java with Swing. The player moves around the screen, places damaging puddles, and survives waves of enemies that chase the player. Killing enemies spawns xp orbs that earn upgrades.

## Requirements

- Java 14 or newer (the code uses switch expressions)
- These image files, either next to the `.java` files or inside the jar:
  - `player.png`
  - `galaxy_puddle.png`
  - `fastenemy.png`
  - `tankenemy.png`
- These image files will automatically be near the exe if you directly extract the zip, so don't fret too much
- If you're just running the exe from the zip you don't need to install Java, it comes bundled with it. The Java requirement is only for running from source

If an image is missing, the game still runs. The player and puddles fall back to drawn shapes, enemies draw without an icon, and the missing file's name is printed to the console.

## Running from source

From the project folder:

```
javac *.java
java Player
```

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| W, A, S, D | Move |
| Left mouse button | Place a puddle |
| Space | Super: dash towards your cursor while placing weaker puddles as you go |
| E | Cast the aura (only once you've unlocked it, see below) |
| P | Pause or resume |
| Enter | Open the upgrade menu when an upgrade is ready |
| 1, 2, 3 | Choose an upgrade while the menu is open(can also click, will also close the menu) |
| R | Restart (only works after game over) |

## How the game works
### Puddles

- Clicking places a puddle at the mouse position. If the click is farther than 500 pixels from the player, the puddle is placed 500 pixels away in the direction of the click.
- A puddle does not land immediately. A warning outline is shown first. The delay is 10 frames for a click at the player's feet and up to 60 frames at maximum range.
- When a puddle lands, it damages every enemy inside it once. After that it damages enemies inside it every 30 frames.
- A puddle lasts 300 frames (5 seconds) after landing.
- Puddles can be placed once every 30 frames.
- Standing in a landed puddle heals the player 20 HP once per second, up to full hp (no overheal has been implemented yet but is certainly in the books).

### Super

- Hitting enemies will each charge a yellow circle around your character. This is your super charge bar, a bar that indicates how much charge you've accumulated via hits for your super.
- Holding space will make the player dash towards your cursor at a speed double to your WASD movement. Additionally, puddles(smaller and less damaging than the ones placed by your mouse, but will heal the same amount) will be placed beneath you as you move. This is intended to be your main form of movement, kinda like a dash that you have to keep better track of rather than just spamming out for movement. 
- You can still use WASD to orient yourself during the super, but it's at a rate of 0.25 your normal WASD, so it's just for orientation rather than conquering your super movement. Use it to avoid enemies as you dash past them. I understand that dashing to your cursor is somewhat of a learning curve for some (My friend struggled with it but maybe he's just a little lost lmao), so use this to bail you out of misplaced cursors.
- Upon reaching your cursor, **YOUR SUPER WILL NOT STOP.** Please be careful of this. You won't place any puddles without actually moving, and you won't be moving, so you're just stuck in your super doing nothing. It won't drain while you sit on the cursor, but you can't place puddles with the mouse until you let go of space either. Be aware of this and try to keep your cursor moving while you super, or at least stop before you reach it.
- As the circle implies, there is a max amount of super you can hold at once. Try to keep using it to reposition and such so any extra hits aren't just wasted.
- It is entirely possible to hypermax upgrading the super drain reducing upgrade until you can infinitely super, if you so desire. 

- Tip: Dashing directly away from your enemies will cause them to walk through your puddle trail, so despite it dealing less damage, you can still make use of it in dpsing down crowds.

### Milestone wave and aura

- At 500 kills, a wave bursts out from the player and kills every enemy on screen. The game keeps going, but enemies stop spawning for 15 seconds so you get a little breather.
- The wave also unlocks the aura. Press E to cast it. It follows you around for 6 seconds (then has a 15 second cooldown), and a thin blue pulse goes out from you to the edge of it every 3 seconds so you can see how far it reaches.
- Enemies inside the aura are slowed to half speed and get hit every half second. The more times the aura hits the same enemy, the more damage each hit does, so it's best on enemies you can't shake off. Enemies that leave the aura go back to normal speed and their hit count resets.
- Like everything else, this resets when you restart.
- This feature at the moment is vibecoded and solely edited by hand rather than being fully written by hand. Sorry about that, I struggled enormously to get this to work in the wake of some other issues in my life. I'll try to avoid doing this again.
### Player

- Starts with 100 HP.
- Touching an enemy deals that enemy's damage, followed by 60 frames of immunity to contact damage.
- At 0 HP the game ends and stops updating. Press R to restart.
- moves 8 pixels per frame, this is the scale that speed stats will be standardized around

### Enemies
- Enemies spawn just outside a random edge of the screen and move toward the player.
- There are three types: the basic enemy, `FastEnemy`, and `TankEnemy`. Fast and tank enemies draw an icon in the middle of the circle.
- Each enemy has a health bar and flashes red briefly when damaged.
- Enemies spawn in groups. Spawn rate, group size (1 to 4), speed, and the share of fast and tank enemies increase over 10 minutes of play. At most 30 enemies exist at once.
- Enemies now scale in damage and hp over time, as well as increasingly scale in speed. The amount each enemy scales in each stat is individualized.

### Enemy types/stats
- Basic Enemy stats: spawns 80% of the time at the start, going down to 50% at max difficulty. 100 hp, 40 dmg, 3-7 speed (increases over time and has small algorithmic variance), white with no icon
- Fast enemies spawn 20% of the time. 60 hp, 1.5x basic enemy speed, 30 dmg, slightly smaller and blue with a speed icon
- Tank enemies spawn 0% of the time at the start, going up to 30% at max difficulty. 160 hp, 0.8x speed, 50 dmg, slightly larger and gray with a shield icon

### Upgrades

- Xp counts using the xp bar on the bottom of the screen until the next upgrade. The xp needed grows by half a point for every xp gained so far.
- When the count reaches 0, the screen shows a prompt. Press Enter to open the menu. The game pauses while the menu is open.
- Current upgrades: bigger puddles (+10 radius, can be bought twice), puddle damage (+5, and each purchase adds 5 more than the last), maximum player health (+20, and each purchase adds 10 more than the last), fleet feet (+2 movement speed, can be bought 3 times), more super charge held (+10%), less super drained (-10%), more hp healed from puddles (+10%), longer puddle place range (+80 pixels), faster puddle throwing (+20% faster but capped at 8 frames), xp magnet (xp orbs
are pulled towards you from +100 more pixels than normal) and healing orbs (+5 healing for collecting an xp orb). Some of these have a limit to how many times they can be purchased. 
- There are some unique upgrades that aren't just stat buffs that can be purchased once each run. Aftershock: puddles explode when expiring, dealing half damage in a 1.5x larger radius. Thorns: Enemies take damage when making contact with the player. Second wind: Once per run, when you take a fatal hit, the hit is cancelled out, you're restored to half of your max hp, and are given 5 seconds of invulnerability.

- **Upgrades reset on restart. This game is purely a roguelike for now, you will take nothing with you between lives except skill and pure passion, I guess**

## Settings

Gameplay values are constants at the top of `PlayerPanel` in `Player.java`. Timers are measured in frames. Game logic runs 60 steps per second based on elapsed time, so the speed does not depend on the machine's timer resolution.

## Files

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `Player.java` | The window, the game loop, input, upgrades, spawning, the aura, and drawing. Despite the name, this file still holds most of the game. |
| `Enemy.java` | The base enemy class: position, HP, movement, collision checks, icon loading, and drawing. |
| `FastEnemy.java` | Subclass of `Enemy` with lower HP, smaller size, and higher speed. |
| `TankEnemy.java` | Subclass of `Enemy` with higher HP, larger size, and lower speed. |
| `Upgrade.java` | Interface implemented by each upgrade. |
| `Puddle.java` | The main attack of the player. Has been migrated out of player because the file has frankly gotten far too large to navigate.
| `Orb.java` | The experience orbs that drop when enemies die. Has been migrated for the same reason as Puddle.

## Packaging for Windows

Run these from the project folder:

```
mkdir out
javac -d out *.java
copy *.png out
jar --create --file BerryBlast.jar --main-class Player -C out .
java -jar BerryBlast.jar
```

To make a folder containing an exe and a bundled Java runtime (requires a JDK with `jpackage`):

```
mkdir jarinput
move BerryBlast.jar jarinput
jpackage --type app-image --name BerryBlast --input jarinput --main-jar BerryBlast.jar --main-class Player --dest dist
```

Zip the whole `dist\BerryBlast` folder and send it. The exe only runs on Windows. Windows may show a "protected your PC" warning because the exe is not signed. Choose More info, then Run anyway.
